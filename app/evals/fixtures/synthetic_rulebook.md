# FINRA Synthetic Rulebook

## Rule 1000. General Standards

### Rule 1000(a). Written Policies

Members must maintain written policies reasonably designed to supervise regulated activity. The policies must identify accountable owners, review frequency, and escalation channels.

### Rule 1000(b). Annual Review

Members must review supervisory policies at least annually. The review must document material changes, unresolved exceptions, and the approval of a designated principal.

## Rule 1010. Communications with Retail Investors

### Rule 1010(a). Fair and Balanced Statements

Retail communications must be fair, balanced, and not misleading. A communication may describe benefits only when it gives equal prominence to material risks and limitations.

### Rule 1010(b). Prohibited Promissory Language

Members must not state or imply that an investment result is guaranteed unless the guarantee is expressly permitted by rule or statute. Hypothetical performance must be clearly labeled and accompanied by assumptions.

### Rule 1010(c). Required Disclosure Table

When a retail communication includes a fee comparison, the member must disclose the comparison period, included charges, and material exclusions.

| Disclosure Item | Required Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Comparison period | Start date and end date |
| Included charges | Advisory fee, platform fee, transaction fee |
| Exclusions | Taxes, third-party charges, unusual expenses |

## Rule 1020. Suitability and Risk Review

### Rule 1020(a). Customer Profile

Before recommending a transaction or strategy, a member must make reasonable efforts to obtain the customer's investment profile. The profile includes age, investment objective, risk tolerance, liquidity needs, time horizon, and financial situation.

### Rule 1020(b). High-Risk Product Review

Recommendations involving complex or high-risk products require documented review by a qualified supervisor before execution. The review must consider concentration, liquidity, leverage, and customer experience.

## Rule 1030. Escalation and Recordkeeping

### Rule 1030(a). Exception Escalation

Employees must escalate suspected violations, unresolved supervisory exceptions, and customer complaints within two business days. The escalation record must identify the issue, responsible owner, and expected resolution date.

### Rule 1030(b). Retention Period

Records required by this rulebook must be retained for six years unless a longer period is required by applicable law. Records must be searchable by customer, account, rule, and date.

## Rule 1040. Governance and Model Use

### Rule 1040(a). Automated Compliance Tools

Members using automated compliance tools must validate the tool before production use and after material changes. Validation evidence must include test cases, limitations, approval records, and monitoring procedures.

### Rule 1040(b). Human Review

Automated compliance tools must not replace required supervisory judgment. A qualified reviewer must evaluate low-confidence alerts, unresolved exceptions, and outputs used for disciplinary decisions.
